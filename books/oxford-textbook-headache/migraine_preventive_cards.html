Preventive treatment of migraine

Who to treat, which drug to choose, how long to continue, and where CGRP antibodies, botulinum toxin and neuromodulation fit.

Core message: no single drug works for everyone. Choose by comorbidity, tolerability and risk.

Based on Chapter 15, Part 21/15
Indications

When to start prevention

Up to 25% of people with migraine are candidates. Individualise the decision.

  • More than 2–3 attacks per month
  • Acute therapy ineffective, or to prevent medication overuse
  • Disabling attacks even if infrequent
  • Severe or disabling aura: no acute drug treats aura
  • Frequency, duration, disability and comorbidity all weigh in
Try first
Lifestyle changes, behavioural approaches, and fixing acute-drug overuse before starting a preventive.
Open question
Early prevention may not stop progression to chronic migraine: topiramate did not prevent it despite beating placebo (INTREPID).
Preventive effects are sustained, so side effects can be too2/15

At least 3 months at a tolerated dose. Stop sooner if poorly tolerated.

Antihypertensives

Beta blockers

Propranolol, metoprolol, nadolol, timolol, atenolol, bisoprolol. Strong evidence. Mechanism unknown; likely reduced cortical excitability (also suppresses CSD in rodents).

  • Propranolol: >20 placebo-controlled trials, meta-analysis of >600 patients; similar to metoprolol, flunarizine, amitriptyline, candesartan
  • Dropout from adverse events <5%
  • Selectivity and lipophilicity do not seem to matter for efficacy
Contraindications
Asthma, insulin-dependent diabetes. Relative: glaucoma, prostatic hypertrophy.
Side effects
Fatigue, bradycardia, low BP, sexual dysfunction. Depression risk unconfirmed.
Best fit
Patients with hypertension or baseline tachycardia.
Stroke note
Non-selective blockers may carry more stroke risk than β1-selective ones (BP variability). Avoid in older patients or those at stroke risk.
Typical doses: propranolol 120–240 mg/day, metoprolol 47.5–200 mg/day5/15
Antihypertensives

Candesartan

Selective AT1 angiotensin II receptor blocker (a prodrug). AT1 receptors in brain and spinal cord modulate pain transmission.

  • Crossover trial, 16 mg/day: fewer headache and migraine days and better 50% responder rate than placebo
  • Later trial: efficacy similar to propranolol, both better than placebo
  • Dizziness and paraesthesias more common than with propranolol
  • Telmisartan was not effective, so this is not a class effect
Consider in
Migraine with hypertension, or patients who tolerate other preventives poorly.
Avoid in pregnancy
Fetal toxicity (category D).
Guideline level C (2012); evidence has since strengthened6/15

Other agents

Flunarizine, naproxen, butterbur

Flunarizine 10 mg/day

  • Calcium channel blocker; 6 fair-to-poor trials beat placebo; equal to pizotifen
  • Sedation, weight gain, depression, extrapyramidal symptoms

Naproxen sodium 1100 mg/day

  • 3 trials beat placebo; comparable to propranolol and pizotifen; also used short term for menstrual migraine
  • Caution with peptic ulcer or GI bleeding; long-term use raises MI and stroke risk
  • Paradox: NSAIDs can cause medication overuse headache when used often as acute therapy

Butterbur (Petasites) 75 mg q12h

  • Root extract: 75 mg (not 50 mg) beat placebo over 4 months
Liver toxicity
Plant components are hepatotoxic. Only the specific purified, patented extract studied was free of reports. Avoid unregulated products.

Supplements

Riboflavin and coenzyme Q10

Rationale: correct a hypothesised deficit in mitochondrial energy metabolism (unproven in migraine).

Riboflavin 400 mg/dayCoQ10 100 mg q8h
AdultsOne 55-patient trial beat placebo; another found no difference between 400 mg + magnesium/feverfew and 25 mg alone42-patient RCT: 50% responders 47.6% vs 14.3% placebo. Liquid preparation used
ChildrenNo benefit (200 mg; 50 mg)No benefit (n = 122)
TolerabilityExceptionalExceptional, low cost
Interpretation
Evidence is weak. They are commonly used because they are safe and cheap, not because they are proven. Unknown whether liquid is better than tablet, or whether dose matters.
Practical
Reasonable add-on, or when drugs are contraindicated (pregnancy planning, polypharmacy). Set expectations honestly.
Guideline: riboflavin B; CoQ10 C10/15
Chronic migraine

Botulinum toxin and topiramate

Chronic migraine: ≥15 headache days/month, ≥8 with migraine features. Onabotulinum toxin A and topiramate are the two agents with specific trial evidence.

  • Botulinum toxin: failed in episodic migraine. Two RCTs (PREEMPT; n = 679 and 705), 155–195 U every 12 weeks; both reduced headache days; second met primary end point
  • No significant fall in acute drug use, even though headache days fell
  • Topiramate 100 mg: two large RCTs positive for headache days, including patients with medication overuse; acute drug use did not fall
  • Valproate and amitriptyline help chronic daily headache (mixed definitions)
Toxin advantage
Only every 12 weeks.
Toxin drawback
Clinic visit; underreported headache worsening and flu-like symptoms.
Controversy
Whether to withdraw overused acute drugs before starting any preventive is unresolved.
11/15
CGRP

CGRP monoclonal antibodies

First migraine-specific preventives. CGRP rises in jugular blood during attacks, normalises with sumatriptan, and CGRP infusion triggers migraine-like attacks.

DrugTarget / routeKey trial dosing
ErenumabCGRP receptor, SC70 or 140 mg monthly (episodic, chronic)
FremanezumabCGRP ligand, SC225 mg monthly or 675 mg quarterly
GalcanezumabCGRP ligand, SC120 or 240 mg monthly (chronic: 240 mg load, then 120 mg)
EptinezumabCGRP ligand, IVPhase II: 1000 mg
  • All trials met primary end points; ~10,000 patients; no clear serious drug-related events
  • Main adverse event: injection-site reaction
Open questions
No head-to-head trials versus older drugs, no response predictors, high cost, limited real-world data. May be superior for some responders.

Neuromodulation

Non-drug devices

DeviceEvidence
Supra-orbital stimulation (20 min daily, 3 months)Sham-controlled RCT (n = 67): fewer migraine days and better 50% responder rate. Survey: 53.4% kept the device, 46.6% returned it
Single-pulse TMS (approved for acute treatment of migraine with aura)Open-label data (n = 263): fewer headache days in episodic and chronic migraine. Uncontrolled

Pros
No systemic side effects. Useful if drugs are contraindicated or poorly tolerated.
Cons
Daily time commitment; discomfort; skin irritation, sleepiness and headache reported; preventive evidence is thinner than for drugs.
