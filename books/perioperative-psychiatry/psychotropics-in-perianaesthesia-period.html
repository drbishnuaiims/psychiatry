💉 Psychotropic Medications in the Perianesthesia Period

Bedside / grand-round reference · Source: Trigo-Blanco & Oprea. Ch. 5, Perioperative Psychiatry (Springer, 2019), pp. 51–63 · Aid only; individualise every patient.

Overview & Master Rules

Core messageContinue psychotropics perioperatively – stopping risks relapse, discontinuation syndromes and post-op confusion. Exceptions needing a plan: MAOIs (and some lithium decisions). Anaesthetists should anticipate interactions; prefer short-acting agents; optimise comorbidities.
  • Up to 10% of the population has a psychiatric illness; WHO 2001: ~450 million with mental / brain disorder; 1 in 4 meet criteria sometime in life → common in surgical patients, often with cardiovascular and other comorbidity ↑ perioperative events.
  • Patient-specific plan: preoperative risk evaluation, optimise comorbidities, short-acting induction / maintenance agents, review psychotropic–anaesthetic interactions.

SSRIs, SNRIs & Serotonin Syndrome

Antidepressant groups: SSRIs / SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs, atypicals. Continue all except MAOIs (relative safety; discontinuation syndrome; loss of effect). Drug lists: SSRIs – fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, fluvoxamine, citalopram, escitalopram · TCAs – amitriptyline, nortriptyline, clomipramine, imipramine, desipramine, protriptyline, doxepin · MAOIs – phenelzine, tranylcypromine, isocarboxazid, selegiline, moclobemide · SNRIs – duloxetine, venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, levomilnacipran · atypicals – bupropion, amoxapine, mirtazapine, trazodone, vilazodone, vortioxetine.

SSRIs

  • Block neuronal 5-HT reuptake; most prescribed in USA; nominal anticholinergic effects (except paroxetine); rare conduction change; less sedating, safer in overdose than TCAs. Side effects: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, sleep disturbance, irritability, tremor, headache, sexual dysfunction.
Serotonin syndromeExcess synaptic 5-HT in brainstem / spinal cord, from combined serotonergic drugs (MAOIs, TCAs, meperidine, tramadol, dextromethorphan, ginseng, St John’s wort) or overdose. Triad: altered mental status (agitation, delirium, confusion) · neuromuscular excess (myoclonus, rigidity, hyperreflexia, clonus) · autonomic instability (hyperthermia, tachycardia, labile BP, diarrhoea). Seizures, rhabdomyolysis, renal failure, arrhythmia, coma, death. Vs NMS: more myoclonus and GI symptoms.
  • Treatment: supportive for hyperthermia / autonomic dysfunction; severe → ICU ± ventilation; cyproheptadine (5-HT2A antagonist; enteral only).
  • Opioids: fentanyl and methadone have serotonergic activity. In patients on scheduled serotonergic drugs avoid meperidine, methadone, tramadol, fentanyl and derivatives; safer: morphine, hydromorphone, codeine, oxycodone, buprenorphine.

Other SSRI issues

  • Discontinuation syndrome: nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, insomnia, vivid dreams, sweating, headache, anxiety, irritability – with any antidepressant; gradual taper advised.
  • Bleeding: ↓ platelet aggregation at higher doses; ↑ risk with NSAIDs; high-bleeding-risk surgery (neuro, cardiac, vascular, spinal) → more transfusion, no mortality change. If high-risk procedure or other coagulation-altering drugs: consider taper weeks ahead or change regimen.
  • CYP: SSRIs inhibit P450 (Table 5.3); CYP2D6 converts codeine → morphine, and oxycodone / hydrocodone → active forms – fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline may reduce analgesia. SSRIs may cause SIADH.

SNRIs

  • Duloxetine: small dopamine reuptake effect; MDD, GAD, neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia, chemo-neuropathy. Side effects (10–20%): nausea, somnolence, insomnia, dizziness. Continue; SSRI precautions.
  • Venlafaxine / desvenlafaxine: no α-adrenergic, cholinergic, histamine effect; no P450 change → no significant anaesthetic interactions; continue; care with serotonergic agents.
  • Levomilnacipran: high NE : 5-HT ratio; nausea, dizziness, insomnia, tachycardia, hypertension; inhibits BACE-1 (possible Alzheimer’s target); continue, serotonergic caution.

Tricyclic Antidepressants & MAOIs

TCAs

  • Uses: depression, neuropathic pain, enuresis, migraine. Block NE + 5-HT reuptake; also muscarinic, histaminic, α-adrenergic antagonism → tachycardia, dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, constipation; postural hypotension; sedation (commonest) and fatigue. ECG: T-wave change, ↑ QRS, bundle branch block, ↑ QTc, PVCs.
  • Highly protein-bound (↑ effect with other highly bound drugs or low protein); hepatic metabolism, active metabolites. Overdose = “three Cs”: cardiotoxicity, convulsions, coma → supportive/ICU; IV sodium bicarbonate.
Anaesthesia for TCA patientContinue (stopping → discontinuation or relapse), with vigilance for catecholamine sensitivity.
  • Avoid: direct (epinephrine, norepinephrine) and indirect (ephedrine, metaraminol) sympathomimetics → hypertension / arrhythmia. Caution with catecholamine-raising ketamine, pancuronium, meperidine.
  • Ventricular arrhythmia risk if hypercapnia with volatile agents (esp. halothane). Exaggerated anticholinergic response; atropine (crosses BBB) → post-op confusion. Potentiate barbiturates, BZDs, opioids. Chronic TCA → depleted catecholamines → cardiac depression, hypotension.
  • Usually continue, esp. no cardiac disease; if arrhythmia concern, taper slowly.

MAOIs

  • Now mostly treatment-resistant depression (side effects, dietary limits). Irreversible MAO inhibition; MAO-A (NE, epinephrine, 5-HT; main brain form) vs MAO-B (phenylethylamine; ~75% of systemic activity, GI, platelets). Dopamine and tyramine metabolised by both. Tyramine foods: aged cheese, cured meat, pickled herring, chicken liver, chocolate. Selegiline: possible slowing of Parkinson’s.
Traditional: stop MAOI 2–3 weeks before elective surgery (enzyme regeneration)
But: discontinuation syndrome + relapse / suicide risk (severe depression) → generally discourage stopping
Decide individually: anaesthesiologist + psychiatrist + patient
If stopped: minimum period, gradual dose cut, psychiatric supervision, restart ASAP post-op. If continued: tyramine-free diet pre-op, close haemodynamic monitoring (serotonin syndrome, hypertensive crisis), also emergencies

Mood Stabilisers

RuleDo not stop abruptly – significant relapse risk. Lithium and anticonvulsants are standard of care in bipolar disorder.

Lithium

  • Gold standard: acute mania, depression, maintenance. Mimics sodium in neuronal membranes; ↓ dopamine / glutamate release, ↑ GABA. Low therapeutic index.
  • Target: older advice 0.8–1.2 mEq/L; modern 0.4–0.8 mEq/L for acute and maintenance. >1.5 mmol/L toxic: nausea, vomiting, lethargy / agitation, polydipsia, polyuria, ataxia, hypokalaemia; severe → renal failure, convulsions, coma, death.
  • Toxicity management: supportive – seizure control, hydration, electrolytes. Haemodialysis best if stable; CVVHD / CVVHDF if haemodynamically unstable. RRT at any level if low consciousness, seizures, life-threatening complications; level >4 mEq/L with ↓ clearance (creatinine >2 mg/dL); or any level >5 mEq/L.
Minor surgery → continue lithium
Major surgery → some stop 24–48 h before (controversial); interruption may precipitate mood relapse
If stopped → restart 24 h post-op
Renal / fluid care: lithium is renally excreted – avoid renal-toxic drugs, NSAIDs ↑ lithium (aspirin is the exception); watch fluid and electrolytes; monitor levels throughout
Anaesthetic effects↓ anaesthetic requirement (blocks brainstem NE / dopamine release). Prolongs depolarising and non-depolarising neuromuscular blockade (↓ ACh synthesis / release, sodium competition) → reduce relaxant dose; use nerve stimulator.

Uses: schizophrenia, schizoaffective, bipolar, adjunct in MDD, irritability in ASD, antiemetic (somatic).

  • Typicals: EPS (dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism – substantia nigra D2 block); antiemetic effect via area postrema; low-potency → anticholinergic effects, sedation, α1 block → orthostatic hypotension; ECG: QT / PR prolongation, T flattening, ST depression, rare PVC / torsades. Inhalational anaesthetics + phenothiazines both prolong QTc → arrhythmia risk.
  • Atypicals: D2 block + 5-HT2 antagonism → less EPS; H1, muscarinic, α-adrenergic effects. Clozapine: ↓ seizure threshold, fatal cardiopulmonary suppression, myocarditis, agranulocytosis.
NMSIdiosyncratic: hyperpyrexia, rigidity, autonomic instability; ↑ CK and WBC; mortality up to 20%; critical care; dantrolene + bromocriptine + supportive. Resembles malignant hyperthermia, but no proven association.

Anaesthetic considerations

  • Continue. Stopping <72 h pre-op → psychosis recurrence and ↑ post-op confusion.
  • Potentiate hypotensive and sedative effects of general anaesthetics; impaired temperature regulation (hypothalamic D2 block).
  • Patients appear less sensitive to post-op pain (NMDA dysregulation in schizophrenia + antipsychotic analgesia).
  • ↑ post-op paralytic ileus (sympathetic hyperactivity; reduced by epidural analgesia); ↑ water intoxication (↑ ADH); sudden death reported (phenothiazine overdose cardiotoxicity).

Benzodiazepines and P450 Interactions

Benzodiazepines

  • Adjuncts for anxiety (SSRIs first line), akathisia, muscle relaxation, hypnosis. Chronic users: continue (avoid withdrawal). Positive allosteric modulators at GABA receptors – the main target of IV anaesthetics; midazolam is the usual pre-induction anxiolytic (diazepam, lorazepam occasionally).
DrugMetabolism
MidazolamCYP3A4 / 3A5 → 1-hydroxymidazolam (main), 4-hydroxy, 1,4-hydroxy. Azole antifungals ↑ levels; oral dose especially (↓ first pass)
DiazepamCYP2C19 + 3A4 (≈80%); active nordazepam (also from chlordiazepoxide), half-life >96 h, accumulates. Fluvoxamine (2C19 inhibitor) ↑ half-life
LorazepamConjugated to inactive water-soluble glucuronide, renal excretion; valproate ↓ its clearance
  • Potency vs diazepam: midazolam 3–6×, lorazepam 5–10×. All have hypnotic, sedative, anxiolytic, amnestic, anticonvulsant, muscle-relaxant actions with rising dose.
  • Synergy with opioids and IV anaesthetics (CNS depression). Acute BZD ↓ inhalational anaesthetic requirement; chronic BZD users need higher concentrations.

Take-Home Points & Revision

Take-home (chapter)
  • Except MAOIs, continue antidepressants; risk marginal
  • Serotonin syndrome: combined serotonergic drugs or overdose; commonest analgesic triggers tramadol and meperidine
  • MAOIs need careful planning; meperidine, ephedrine, metaraminol absolutely contraindicated
  • Moclobemide = reversible MAO-A, better safety
  • Continue mood stabilisers and antipsychotics
  • Lithium: narrow index – monitor levels, fluids, avoid renal injury
Numbers
  • MAOI stop 2–3 wk; moclobemide 24 h; amoxapine 24 h
  • Selegiline <10 mg/day: continue
  • Lithium target 0.4–0.8; toxic >1.5; RRT >4 (↓ clearance) / >5
  • Lithium hold 24–48 h; restart 24 h post-op
  • Antipsychotic stop <72 h → confusion
  • NMS mortality ≤20%
  • Midazolam 3–6×, lorazepam 5–10× diazepam
Drug to avoid / care
  • TCA: ephedrine, epinephrine, ketamine, pancuronium, meperidine, atropine
  • MAOI: ketamine, pancuronium, cocaine, indirect sympathomimetics
  • Li: NSAIDs; reduce NMB dose
  • Valproate: needs more rocuronium / vecuronium
  • Carbamazepine: induces 3A4
  • Bupropion + ketamine: seizure
Self-test
  • Patient on sertraline needs codeine post-op? → CYP2D6 inhibition ↓ analgesia; use morphine / hydromorphone.
  • Patient on phenelzine, hypotensive after induction? → fluids, then phenylephrine 25–50 µg; not ephedrine.
  • Lithium patient, rocuronium dose? → reduce and use nerve stimulator.
  • Which opioids are safer with serotonergic antidepressants? → morphine, hydromorphone, codeine, oxycodone, buprenorphine.
  • Patient on valproate – NMB? → resistance to rocuronium / vecuronium.
