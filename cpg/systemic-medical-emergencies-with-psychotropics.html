🩺 Systemic Medical Emergencies with Psychotropics

Bedside / grand-round reference · Source: Grover, Sarkar, Avasthi. Indian J Psychiatry 2022;64(Suppl 2):S252–S278 · Aid only; individualise every patient.

General Approach

Core ideaMany of these are rare, nonspecific, and often multiple in one patient. Needs a high index of suspicion; association is judged by temporal link, literature, dechallenge, rechallenge. Diagnosis stays provisional until the syndrome resolves. (NMS, serotonin, dystonia are covered in the sister guideline.)

Flow

Patient + suspected side effect
History: features, comorbidity, start/change/removal of drugs (removing an inhibitor can raise levels), prior episodes
Assess causality (Naranjo / WHO-UMC scale)
Examine (vitals, systems, MSE/delirium) + investigations
Vitals; choose setting: OPD / ward / ICU
Stop offending drug – weigh risk-benefit, severity, causality, need for the drug (with the relevant specialist)
Treat organ problem by severity → later choose a safer psychotropic

Key history prompts

  • Dates: onset, drug start, dose change, dechallenge / rechallenge, other new drugs, adherence
  • Risk comorbidity: DM, HTN, hypothyroid, COPD, heart failure, head injury, stroke, cirrhosis, malignancy
  • Drug interactions, OTC / herbals, substances, plasma levels
  • Is it secondary to another side effect? (e.g. hyponatremia → seizure; hyponatremia → arrhythmia)

Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma

EmergencySustained ↑IOP → irreversible optic nerve / RNFL damage → blindness.

Mechanism: pupillary block via adrenergic / anticholinergic action (or idiosyncratic). Drugs: TCAs, MAOIs, phenothiazines & other anticholinergic antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, topiramate, SSRIs (paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine), SNRIs.

Presentation
  • Sudden vision loss, eye pain, red eye
  • Headache, blur, tunnel vision, halos around lights
  • Nausea / vomiting
  • Mid-dilated pupil
Risk factors
  • Age >60, female
  • Inuit, Asian, Hispanic
  • Narrow angle, shallow chamber, hyperopia, small eye, thin cornea
  • Prior angle closure / family history
  • DM, heart disease, HTN, sickle cell, trauma

Tests: tonometry (IOP), slit lamp, gonioscopy, ophthalmoscopy, visual fields, UBM.

ManagementStop offending drug · topical β-blocker, α2-agonist, prostaglandin analogues · laser peripheral iridotomy by severity. Prevent: avoid strongly anticholinergic / adrenergic drugs in vulnerable people; regular eye review.

Clozapine neutropenia (ANC) pathway

Mild 1000–1500/µL · do not stop · ANC 3×/week until >1500
Moderate 500–1000/µL · stop clozapine · ANC daily until >1000, then 3×/week until >1500, then weekly × 4 weeks
Severe <500/µL · stop · G-CSF with haematologist · avoid re-exposure · treat infection

For non-clozapine drugs: no clear guideline – stop suspect agent until cause established. Investigate: CBC, blood film, marrow biopsy if needed.

Venous thromboembolism

  • Linked to FGAs and SGAs (SGAs higher): chlorpromazine, haloperidol, prochlorperazine, clozapine, olanzapine, risperidone; aripiprazole generally not; quetiapine only case reports. Also TCAs, SSRIs.
  • Risks: immobility, hormones, obesity, age, varicose veins, dehydration, thrombophilia.
  • DVT: leg pain / swelling / redness / warmth · PE: dyspnoea, tachypnoea, tachycardia, chest pain.
  • Tests: compression / duplex US, MRI, venography; CTPA / V-Q scan for PE.
  • Stop agent; IV unfractionated heparin; monitor INR.

Hyponatremia

Commonest with SSRIs (0.06–40%), then carbamazepine (4.8–41.5%, small studies), antipsychotics, rarely BZDs / Z-drugs. TCAs lower than SSRIs. Usual mechanism: SIADH.

Risk factors
  • Older age, female (antidepressants), weight <60 kg
  • Diuretics, polypharmacy, CYP450 inhibitors
  • Prior hyponatremia, baseline Na <138
  • DM, HTN, hypothyroid, COPD, CCF, cirrhosis
  • Summer season; early phase of treatment
Differential
  • Psychogenic polydipsia
  • Cardiac / renal / hepatic failure
  • Dehydration
  • Pseudo-hyponatremia (↑glucose, ↑TG, ↓protein)
  • Hypothyroid, hypoadrenalism, tumour / CNS SIADH

Tests: serum Na + electrolytes, urine Na (>30 → SIADH), urine osmolality (>100), glucose, lipids, protein, RFT, LFT, ECG; MRI if central pontine myelinolysis suspected.

Management

Stop suspected psychotropic · daily Na until normal
Mild
Stop drug; if no rise → water restriction 0.5–1 L/day
Moderate
Stop drug + water restriction; hypertonic saline if neuro signs
Severe
Hypertonic 3% saline (with physician) + furosemide
Not resolving → vasopressin antagonists (conivaptan / tolvaptan)
  • 3% saline 1 mL/kg/h until improvement and Na ↑ 4–6 mEq/L; seizure / coma: 100 mL 3% bolus.
  • Max correction 10–12 mEq/L in 24 h – fast correction → central pontine myelinolysis.
  • Furosemide prevents kidneys concentrating urine despite high ADH.

Cardiac: QTc, Hypotension, Hypertension

Most cardiac effects are clinically silent, but QTc→torsades, myocarditis, cardiomyopathy, pericarditis carry highest fatality risk. Consider ECG + BP at baseline for all on antipsychotics. Cardiac signs may be secondary (e.g. hyponatremia → arrhythmia).

Hypotension (orthostatic)

  • Criterion: fall of ≥20 mmHg systolic or ≥10 diastolic within 2–5 min of standing.
  • Risk: antihypertensives, nitrates, CCBs, diuretics, α-blockers; autonomic failure (DM, alcohol, Parkinson), dehydration.
  • Complications: syncope → fractures, TIA, stroke, MI.
  • Mild: reduce dose, abdominal binder (on before rising, off at bedtime), compression stockings, fluids 1.25–2.5 L/day, rise slowly, avoid alcohol / heat / carbohydrate-rich meals. Severe: stop the agent.
  • Very often (≥10%): amitriptyline, tranylcypromine, trimipramine, chlorprothixene, flupentixol, levomepromazine, olanzapine.

Hypertension

  • Highest: atomoxetine (≥10%). Often: bupropion, citalopram, duloxetine, milnacipran, reboxetine, tranylcypromine, venlafaxine, methylphenidate.
  • Features: early-morning headache, epistaxis, visual change, tinnitus; severe: nausea, chest pain, confusion. Usually asymptomatic.
  • Confirm with repeated readings, look for other causes, stop suspected drug and monitor; baseline BP before starting.

Clozapine monitoring (first 4 weeks)

  • Baseline: troponin, CRP, ECG, echo, HR, temp, BP.
  • Daily symptoms (fever, chest pain, dyspnoea, myalgia, cough, GI); pulse / BP / RR / temp every 2 days; troponin + CRP weekly.
  • Escalate monitoring: pulse >120 or ↑>30 bpm, CRP 50–100 mg/L, troponin <2× ULN.
  • Stop clozapine if troponin >2× ULN or CRP >100 mg/L.

Hyperammonemic Encephalopathy & DKA

Hyperammonemia (NH₃ >45 µmol/L)

  • Drug: valproate (commonest); also topiramate, lamotrigine, zonisamide, carbamazepine, phenytoin, risperidone, olanzapine. Not clearly dose / duration related; LFTs can be normal.
  • Risks: urea-cycle disorder, carnitine deficiency, high protein load, polypharmacy, poor nutrition, thyroid disease.
  • Features: lethargy, headache, N/V, tremor, myoclonus, parkinsonism, asterixis, ataxia, slurred speech, seizures, drowsiness, confusion, catatonia, agitation, coma.
  • Tests: serum ammonia, EEG / MRI / CT, glutamate, carnitine, urea-cycle work-up.
Stop valproate / agent · hydration · protein restriction · monitor glucose
Lactulose / rifaximin / neomycin · L-carnitine
Severe: furosemide, acetaglutamide (as stated in source), mannitol for oedema · NCG if NAG-synthetase deficiency
Dialysis if NH₃ 300–500 µmol/L

Diabetic ketoacidosis

  • Rare SGA effect (olanzapine & clozapine most; also risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole); mostly first 6 months; antipsychotic polypharmacy; can follow drug-induced pancreatitis.
  • Risks: middle age, male, unrecognised hyperglycaemia / high HbA1c, overweight, weight gain, type 2 DM.
  • Prodrome: weight change, polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia → weakness, fruity breath, N/V (coffee-ground), dehydration, ↓sensorium.
  • Tests: HbA1c, urine / serum ketones, effective osmolality, anion gap.
  • Treat: hydration, electrolytes, insulin, glucose monitoring. Prevent: lower-risk drugs, regular glucose checks.

Aspiration Pneumonia & GI Emergencies

Aspiration pneumonia

  • Meta-analysis risk ratio vs no antipsychotic: FGA 1.69, SGA 1.93 (no significant FGA / SGA difference). Linked: clozapine, risperidone, quetiapine, olanzapine, zotepine, haloperidol. Also BZDs / Z-drugs, antidepressants, valproate, carbamazepine, pregabalin. Lithium possibly protective (bipolar).
  • Risks: old age, dementia, COPD / asthma, dysphagia, smoking, stroke, polypharmacy, steroids, early clozapine phase, underweight, poor dentition, PPI use.
  • Features: cough, dyspnoea, fever, tachypnoea, tachycardia, dull percussion, pleural rub, hypotension, delirium; can progress to respiratory failure / septic shock.
  • Tests: CXR, ABG, sputum / blood culture, CBC. Treat: stop agent, O₂, IV fluids, antibiotics, monitor cardiac status. Prevent: minimum effective dose, avoid polypharmacy, stop smoking / alcohol.

Upper GI bleeding (SSRIs)

  • SSRI OR ~1.4–1.55; also mirtazapine, bupropion. NNH 791 (SSRI alone); 160 with NSAIDs, 294 with antiplatelets.
  • Relative risk 2.06 SSRI alone, 4.60 with low-dose aspirin, 6.95 with NSAID; warfarin also adds risk.
  • Risks: age >80, past UGIB, first month of therapy. Acid suppression lowers risk.
  • Treat: stop drug, stabilise haemodynamics ± transfuse, then endoscopy. Safer: tianeptine, reboxetine, SNRIs, TCAs (+ PPI).

Severe gut hypomotility / paralytic ileus

  • Clozapine (anti-muscarinic + anti-α + anti-5HT), quetiapine, olanzapine, high-potency FGAs, anticholinergics, TCAs. Amisulpride and aripiprazole not linked in one study.
  • Risks: age, first 4 months of clozapine, high dose, opioids, stopping smoking, delayed reporting.
  • Pain, vomiting, constipation, distension, absent bowel sounds → shock / perforation.
  • X-ray / US abdomen; stop drug, supportive, surgery if perforation. No rechallenge. Prevent: treat constipation before clozapine, monitor bowel every visit, fibre, fluids.

Pancreatitis

  • SSRIs (OR 1.26, highest first 2 weeks), clozapine, olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole, ziprasidone, valproate. Most psychotropics = Badalov Class III.
  • Risks: alcohol, DM, gallstones, other pancreatitis drugs, ↑TG, ↓Ca.
  • Epigastric pain → back, fever; amylase, lipase, US / CT / MRCP. Treat: stop drug, haemodynamic stability, antibiotics; avoid rechallenge.

Hepatic Injury & Genitourinary

Drug-induced liver injury (DILI)

  • ~90% hepatocellular; chlorpromazine → cholestasis. Mostly idiosyncratic, not dose-related. Also NAFLD via metabolic syndrome (olanzapine, clozapine).
  • High-risk antidepressants: imipramine, amitriptyline, nefazodone, venlafaxine, duloxetine, sertraline, bupropion, trazodone, agomelatine. Mood stabilisers: carbamazepine, valproate, lamotrigine, topiramate. BZDs: diazepam, chlordiazepoxide, flurazepam.
  • Biochemistry: ALT ≥3× ULN, ALP ≥2×, bilirubin >2 mg/dL. Severe: ↑bilirubin + ↓albumin + INR ≥1.5.
  • Hypersensitivity type: 1–6 weeks, fever, rash, eosinophilia, autoantibodies; idiosyncratic: later, none.
  • Rule out paracetamol, other drugs / herbals, viral and autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson’s, Budd–Chiari, malignancy.
  • Mild: stop drug usually enough; severe: hepatoprotective measures. Prefer drugs less hepatically metabolised: amisulpride, paliperidone; avoid extensive first-pass (venlafaxine, sertraline, bupropion, chlorpromazine, quetiapine).

Priapism

  • Mostly α1-adrenergic blockade; ischaemic (low-flow, painful, rigid corpora, soft glans). Also clitoral priapism.
  • Drugs: phenothiazines, haloperidol, risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, clozapine; trazodone, nefazodone, bupropion, SSRIs; buspirone, hydroxyzine, methylphenidate, atomoxetine.
  • Risk: prior priapism, DM, sickle cell, hypercoagulable, α-blockers, PDE5 inhibitors, lithium, antiretrovirals, cocaine / cannabis / alcohol.
  • Tests: coagulation, corporal blood gas.
Stop offending drug
Step 1 · Penile aspiration until fresh red blood
Step 2 · Intracavernosal phenylephrine (100–500 µg/mL, 1 mL every 3–5 min, max 1 mg/h, monitor vitals)
Step 3 · Penile shunt surgery

Urinary retention

  • Anticholinergic + adrenergic effects: TCAs / SNRIs > SSRIs; phenothiazines, olanzapine, clozapine, carbamazepine, diazepam, baclofen, opioids.
  • Elderly with BPH; other anticholinergics, opioids, α-agonists, NSAIDs, CCBs.
  • Exclude stones, stricture, prostatic disease, infection, trauma, neurological (GBS, cord lesion, DM neuropathy); in women prolapse, pelvic mass, vulvovaginitis.
  • Stop / reduce drug + immediate catheterisation.

Quick Revision

Clozapine checklist
  • ANC schedule: mild >1000 continue; moderate 500–1000 stop; <500 stop + G-CSF
  • Myocarditis: first 2–3 mo; stop if troponin >2× ULN / CRP >100
  • Cardiomyopathy: 6–9 mo
  • Ileus: first 4 mo, stopped smoking
  • Seizures, hyponatremia improves, aspiration, DILI
Thresholds
  • QTc >500 ms → stop
  • Na correction ≤10–12 mEq/L / 24 h
  • Orthostatic: ≥20/10 mmHg
  • NH₃ >45; dialysis 300–500 µmol/L
  • EF <50% = cardiomyopathy
  • ALT ≥3× ULN, ALP ≥2×, bilirubin >2
Drug → problem
  • SSRI → hyponatremia, UGIB, pancreatitis
  • Valproate → hyperammonemia, pancreatitis
  • Trazodone / antipsychotics → priapism
  • Thioridazine / pimozide → TdP
  • Olanzapine / clozapine → DKA
  • Atomoxetine → hypertension
  • Paroxetine / TCAs → glaucoma, retention
Pitfalls
  • Do not label early; diagnose provisional, exclude other causes
  • Stopping co-medications can destabilise physical illness – liaise with specialist
  • Rapid Na correction → osmotic demyelination
  • Avoid rechallenge (hyponatremia, pancreatitis, ileus, severe neutropenia)
  • Check removal of an inhibitor as cause of ↑level
Self-test
  • Halos, eye pain, mid-dilated pupil on paroxetine? → Angle-closure glaucoma; stop drug, urgent IOP lowering / iridotomy.
  • Day 12 of clozapine, fever, ↑troponin? → Myocarditis; stop clozapine.
  • Valproate, lethargy, tremor, normal LFT? → check ammonia.
  • Painful 6-h erection on risperidone + trazodone? → Ischaemic priapism: aspirate, phenylephrine, shunt.
