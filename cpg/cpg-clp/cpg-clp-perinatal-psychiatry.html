🤰 Psychiatric Disorders in the Perinatal Period

Bedside / grand-round reference · Source: Bharadwaj, Endumathi, Parial, Chandra. Indian J Psychiatry 2022;64(Suppl 2):S414–S428 · Aid only; individualise every patient.

Overview & Referral Contexts

Why it mattersWith falling obstetric / medical maternal mortality, maternal mental health is now a major contributor to morbidity and mortality. Relationship with obstetrics is bidirectional.

Referrals come from: (i) antenatal / postnatal OPD, (ii) obstetric in-patient or labour room, (iii) obstetric emergency.

General Approach by Stage

Which stage?
Preconception · folic acid 5 mg/day · consider contraception (till 6–12 months of remission) · delay pregnancy if still symptomatic · trial of discontinuation if single mild past episode
Pregnancy · early, regular antenatal care · anomaly scans at 10–12 wk and 16–18 wk · fetal echo if on lithium / SSRI · deliver in a centre with maternal + neonatal ICU
Lactation · exclusive on-demand breastfeeding · RID <10% preferred · no need to time feeds after dosing · clozapine incompatible · stimulate the infant
Already on medication
  • Monotherapy over polypharmacy
  • Taper adjuncts: BZDs, β-blockers, anticholinergics
  • Never stop abruptly
  • Switching only if current drug carries serious risk
  • SMI: prophylaxis preferred even when well
Starting / restarting
  • Mild: psychotherapy first
  • Choose drug with most safety data and RID <10%
  • Prophylaxis if past SMI episodes
  • New illness in 1st trimester: psychotherapy for mild; drugs if persistent / worsening / severe

Pharmacokinetic changes in pregnancy

  • ↓ gastric emptying, ↑ plasma volume, ↑ volume of distribution (lipophilic), ↑ CYP450 and UGT, ↑ GFR → faster clearance; doses may need adjusting.
DrugChangePrecaution
AntidepressantsLevels may fall after ~20 weeksMonitor symptoms ± drug levels
Lithium↑ GFR + fluid → levels fall; return to pre-pregnancy right after deliveryLevel monthly to 34 wk, then weekly; repeat before and 24 h after delivery if fluid balance poor; frequent in first postpartum weeks
Lamotrigine↓ ~50% (↑ oestrogen, UGT); folic acid may lower effectLevel monitoring preconception → 1st postpartum month

Lactation – infant monitoring

  • Sedatives: sedation, floppiness, respiratory depression, cyanosis.
  • Antidepressants: crying, irritability, diarrhoea, jitteriness, seizures.
  • Antipsychotics: rigidity, poor suck, poor feeding, irritability.
  • Withhold breastfeeding if toxicity; take paediatric advice for preterm / low-birth-weight / ill neonates.
  • Possible mild, reversible developmental delay (commoner in preterm / LBW / older mothers) → advise stimulation: massage, oil bath, lullabies, talking, toys, emotional attachment figure. Risk is outweighed by benefit of treating maternal SMI.

Risk–Benefit & Medication Safety

Four-way weighing(i) risks of untreated illness · (ii) benefits of avoiding exposure · (iii) benefits of good control · (iv) risks of medication. Confounding by indication – some risks come from the illness, not the drug.
  • Untreated illness risks: poor antenatal adherence, self / infant neglect, suicide / infanticide, poor nutrition, domestic violence, substance use, preterm birth, low birth weight, stillbirth.
  • FDA letters (1979–2015) replaced by PLLR (2015): pregnancy, lactation, and females / males of reproductive potential sections.

Whom – woman + primary carer (spouse); representative only if she lacks capacity
How – list options → benefits of prophylaxis (relapse rates) → risks of untreated illness → drug risks

Common Mental Disorders

PrincipleMild episodes → psychotherapy alone (CBT, IPT, ERP for OCD). Add drugs if persistent, recurrent, worsening, or poor response.

Anxiety (infertility, loss, tokophobia, trauma)

  • Psychoeducation, supportive counselling, obstetrician reassurance after check-up → structured therapy → medication.
  • SSRIs (except paroxetine) are first-line drugs. Buspirone (FDA B): little human data.

Depression – prophylaxis decision

Trial of discontinuation OK
  • Single, mild past episode
  • Asymptomatic for 6–12 months
  • Early review for relapse; supportive caregiver
Continue prophylaxis
  • ≥4 lifetime episodes
  • Psychotic depression
  • Suicide attempts during episodes
  • Past hospitalisation or ECT

Risk ↑: domestic violence, past trauma. Protective: good social support. Always rule out bipolarity (hypomania, family history) before an antidepressant.

OCD

  • Trial off SSRI: mild, good prior ERP / CBT response, prolonged remission, no comorbid depression.
  • Continue SSRI if severe, comorbid depression or suicide risk; clomipramine may continue with monitoring; add ERP / CBT.

Severe Mental Illness & Postpartum Disorders

RelapseBipolar: ~66% relapse if prophylaxis stopped vs ~25% if continued. Continue prophylaxis in schizophrenia too.

Bipolar disorder

  • SGA monotherapy preferred (monitor weight, OGTT).
  • Bipolar depression: olanzapine / other SGA; short-term antidepressant with caution; if severe and SGA fails → fluoxetine + olanzapine.
  • Add mood stabiliser only if SGA inadequate: lithium (if needed for wellness) or lamotrigine. Avoid valproate / carbamazepine.
  • Relapse prevention: sleep, stress management, adherence, no substances, early warning signs.

Schizophrenia

  • SGA monotherapy; FGAs and LAIs not started in pregnancy (less data) – continue if stable on FGA and switching risks relapse. LAIs clear over weeks after stopping.
  • Acute: short-term BZD with non-sedating SGA. Remission / negative symptoms: low-dose SGA + psychosocial.

Postpartum depression

  • Onset usually within 4 weeks; risk period up to 2 years. Mild: antidepressant; psychotic: add antipsychotic; high suicide risk: in-patient care. Past PPD → postpartum prophylaxis.
  • Brexanolone (allopregnanolone, GABA-A modulator) for mod–severe PPD, in-patient IV infusion: 30 → 60 → 90 → 60 → 30 µg/kg/h (4 h, 20 h, 28 h, 4 h, 4 h). Side effects: headache, dizziness, syncope. FDA-approved, not launched in India.

Postpartum psychosis (within 4–6 weeks)

Abrupt onset · mania / transient psychosis / catatonia / psychotic depression
Exclude medical mimics; assess suicide + infanticide risk; in-patient care
Breastfeeding-compatible SGA · + antidepressant (psychotic depression) · + mood stabiliser (mania)

Catatonia (≈20% of perinatal admissions vs ~8% general)

Lorazepam 3–8 mg/day (1–2 mg three–four times)
No response in 24–48 h → 8–16 mg/day
Hold if maternal pulse <60, BP <90/60, or fetal HR <100
Taper when possible; start treatment of underlying illness · non-response → ECT

Benefit usually exceeds risk: refusal to eat → dehydration, hypoglycaemia, electrolyte problems; immobility → DVT.

Substance Use · Suicide · Agitation

Alcohol

  • Fetal: growth restriction, facial anomalies, CNS dysfunction. No safe level.
  • Low use: brief intervention / MI. Moderate–high risk: specialist de-addiction.
  • Severe withdrawal: short-course lorazepam or diazepam. Naltrexone / acamprosate: limited data (only if high relapse risk). Disulfiram avoided.

Other substances

  • Cannabis: offspring mood disorders, ASD, inattention-hyperactivity.
  • Tobacco: preterm birth, low birth weight, lip / mouth malformations, maternal bleeding. Psychotherapy first; NRT and bupropion limited but reassuring data – individualise.

Suicide

Key factsSuicide risk is higher in the first year after childbirth than any other time; more lethal methods are used. Predictors: younger age, middle SES, poor perceived support, domestic violence, depressive symptoms, past suicidality. Suicide and infanticide may co-occur.
  • High-intensity in-patient unit; temporary mother–infant separation until risk falls; continue breast milk (expressed / supervised visits); restore joint admission later.
  • ECT if suicidality in depression / psychosis.
  • Family educated; eyeball-to-eyeball monitoring; twice-daily risk review (lethality, intent); remove sharps.
  • She may try to leave with the infant – ensure safety.

Agitation

Verbal de-escalation first
Oral: lorazepam 1–2 mg · promethazine 25–50 mg · olanzapine 5–10 mg · chlorpromazine 50–100 mg
IM: lorazepam 2 mg · promethazine 25–50 mg · haloperidol 2.5–5 mg (with promethazine to prevent dystonia) · olanzapine 10 mg

Monitor temperature, pulse, RR, BP, fetal well-being. Position supine with right hip elevated (avoid aorto-caval compression).

ECT · rTMS · Bonding · Mental Healthcare Act

ECT in pregnancy

Can be life-saving: catatonia or severe suicidality in depression unresponsive to drugs.

Before
  • Obstetric + pre-anaesthetic clearance
  • 8 h fast
  • Avoid anticholinergics (↓ LES tone); oral antacid 15–20 min before
  • Hydrate (NS / Ringer)
During
  • Preoxygenate; avoid hyperventilation
  • Glycopyrrolate (does not cross placenta)
  • Right hip elevated after 20 weeks
  • Fetal Doppler HR before / after; twice-weekly NST
  • Oxytocin release may cause painful contractions → tocolytics if persistent
General / postpartum
  • Setting with emergency obstetric + neonatal care
  • Feed infant before ECT; prioritise mother on list; keep expressed milk available

rTMS

Limited studies; none report adverse maternal or infant outcomes.

Mother–infant bonding

  • Early attachment problems → later internalising / externalising problems. Assess with PBQ + interview.
  • Interventions: education, infant-care training, modelling, video feedback, mother–infant psychotherapy (severe); involve another caregiver (grandmother / father).

Mental Healthcare Act 2017 & MBUs

Least restrictive option: OPD for mild illness
In-patient when moderately severe, for mother / infant safety
Low suicide / infanticide risk → rooming-in of infant (<3 years) – Section 21(2)
If psychiatrist separates mother and baby → review every 15 days; beyond 30 days → inform Mental Health Review Board and seek permission

MBU: in-patient unit with ≥4 beds, rooming-in of mother + infant / child <3 y, run by a multidisciplinary team.

Self-test
