💊 Medical Emergencies with Psychotropics

Bedside / grand-round reference · Source: Grover, Sarkar, Avasthi. Indian J Psychiatry 2022;64(Suppl 2):S236–S250 · Clinical aid, not a substitute for clinical judgement.

General approach to the three syndromes (NMS / SS / anticholinergic)

1 · STOP offending / suspected agent(s)
2 · Confirm diagnosis: history, exam, investigations
3 · Rule out differentials & grade severity
4 · Supportive care (ABC, cooling, fluids, benzodiazepines)
5 · Specific antidote / drug
6 · Prevent complications
7 · Restart psychotropics for primary illness (cautiously)

Acute Dystonia (antipsychotic-induced)

Sudden involuntary sustained contraction → twisting / repetitive movements, abnormal postures; painful. Focal or generalized.

TimingWithin 7 days of starting / rapid dose rise / removing anticholinergic. 95% within 4 days. Prevalence 2–90%. Higher D2 : ACh antagonism → higher risk.

High-potency (haloperidol, fluphenazine, pimozide)Age 10–19MalePrior dystonia (strongest)Family historyCocaineMood disorderHypocalcemia / hypoparathyroidismHyperthyroidismDehydration

Differential

  • Other drugs: metoclopramide, SSRIs, buspirone, diazepam, sumatriptan, chloroquine/HCQ, phenylpropanolamine; cocaine, ecstasy
  • Dissociation, catatonia, tardive dystonia (months–years, no quick anticholinergic response), temporal lobe epilepsy, hypocalcemia

Management – stepwise

Step 1 · IM/IV anticholinergic / antihistamine: Benztropine 1–2 mg · Biperiden 5 mg · Diphenhydramine 25–50 mg
Response: 15–20 min IM, ~5 min IV
Alternative: Diazepam 2–5 mg / Lorazepam 1–2 mg (choice for laryngospasm)
Oculogyric crisis: oral clonazepam 0.5–4 mg/day divided
Step 2 · No response → repeat same drug after ~30 min
Step 3 · Still no response → switch drug
Step 4 · Fails → reconsider diagnosis (e.g. persistent trismus → dislocated jaw)
After resolutionContinue anticholinergic ≥24–48 h (practice: 4–7 days). Routine prophylaxis not recommended – use only if risk factors / high-potency drug / high dose.

Akathisia

Greek: “inability to sit”. Subjective inner restlessness + dysphoria, mounting tension on staying still + objective restlessness.

DangerAssociated with high risk of suicidal behaviour – always assess suicidality.
TypeTiming
AcuteFirst hours–days of antipsychotic
Chronic≥3 months
WithdrawalWithin 6 weeks of dose reduction / stopping
TardiveAfter long-term use

Risks: first exposure, rapid escalation, antipsychotic polypharmacy. Scale: Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale (BARS) – use before starting/increasing dose.

DDx: psychotic/affective agitation, non-akathisia dysphoria, RLS, anxiety, drug withdrawal, delirium/head injury/hypoglycemia, Parkinson’s/Huntington’s, tardive dyskinesia, insomnia.

Confirm diagnosis (history, exam, BARS) + assess suicidality
Reduce dose / switch to low-propensity agent (low-potency FGA or quetiapine)
First line: β-blocker (propranolol) – gold standard
C/I: hypotension, bradycardia, DM, asthma, conduction defects → Mirtazapine (alt. first line) → then Vit B6

Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)

Rare, life-threatening idiosyncratic reaction; reported with almost all antipsychotics (also metoclopramide, mood stabilizers). Incidence 0.02–3.23%. Mechanism: D2 blockade → impaired thermoregulation (↓ heat loss, ↑ heat production).

TetradFever · Lead-pipe rigidity · Altered sensorium · Autonomic instability (↑HR, ↑RR, sweating, labile/high BP, hypersalivation)

Onset: most within 10 days, almost all within 30 days (can occur later). Stage 5 (worst): extreme rigidity, HR 130–150, SBP 140–210, DBP 100–110, temp 39–42 °C, catatonia, coma.

Risk factors

Treatment
  • First week, fast titration, high dose
  • Parenteral, high-potency
  • Polypharmacy, antipsychotic + lithium
Patient
  • Young / old, male
  • Past/family history of NMS
  • Dehydration, malnutrition, iron deficiency, infection, trauma
  • Mood/psychotic disorder, catatonia, agitation
  • Postpartum; hot humid climate; physical restraint

Other drugs linked: sertraline, paroxetine, amitriptyline, lithium, carbamazepine, metoclopramide, antiparkinsonian withdrawal, tetrabenazine.

Diagnosis

DSM-5ALL 3 primary: dopamine-blocker exposure + severe rigidity + fever, PLUS ≥2 of: diaphoresis, dysphagia, tremor, incontinence, altered consciousness, mutism, tachycardia, labile/high BP, leukocytosis, ↑CPK. International Expert Consensus score: cutoff 74.

Treatment

DrugDoseNotes / side effects
Bromocriptine (most used)10–40 mg/day divided, oralHypotension, psychosis
DantroleneOral 50–200 mg/day; IV 2–3 mg/kg/day (max 10 mg/kg/day)Hepatotoxicity
Amantadine100–300 mg BDHepatotoxicity, psychosis, seizures
Levodopa/carbidopa25–250 mg TDS–QIDPsychosis, MI, arrhythmia, dyskinesia
RefractoryConsider ECT. Use succinylcholine cautiously (shared pathophysiology with malignant hyperthermia).
Restart antipsychoticOnly ≥2 weeks after resolution, with close monitoring (recurrence risk).

Serotonin Syndrome (serotonin toxicity)

Extreme end of serotonin toxicity. Seen with ≥2 serotonergic agents, SSRIs + CYP3A4 inhibitors, overdose; severe if MAOI combined with another agent. Onset typically 6–24 h.

Hunter criteria (serotonergic agent + ONE of)

  • Spontaneous clonus
  • Inducible clonus + agitation or diaphoresis
  • Ocular clonus + agitation or diaphoresis
  • Tremor + hyperreflexia
  • Hypertonia + temp >38 °C + ocular or inducible clonus

Sternbach: ≥3 of 10 (mental status change, agitation, myoclonus, hyperreflexia, diaphoresis, shivering, tremor, diarrhea, incoordination, fever); exclude other causes; no recent neuroleptic start/increase.

DDx extra: carcinoid syndrome. Ask about OTC, illicit drugs, ginseng, tryptophan, appetite suppressants.

Management

Stop all serotonergic agents
Mild: supportive care + benzodiazepines
Moderate–severe: Cyproheptadine 12 mg loading (oral/NG) → 2 mg every 2 h until improved → 8 mg 6 h after settling
Severe: ICU, treat hyperthermia / rhabdo / DIC / ARDS; paralysis with vecuronium (non-depolarizing)
AvoidOpioids in management. Restart antidepressant after 1–2 weeks; slow titration; psychoeducate on interacting drugs/supplements.

Anticholinergic Syndrome

ACh antagonism in CNS + periphery (mainly muscarinic). From intentional/accidental intake or therapeutic drugs. No diagnostic criteria – relies on clinical awareness.

Mnemonic pictureDry mouth, dry red hot skin, dilated pupils, urinary retention, ↓bowel sounds, delirium, tachycardia, hyperthermia
SystemFeatures
CentralAgitation, hallucinations, cognitive dysfunction, confusion/delirium, sedation, seizures

CVSTachycardia, arrhythmias, wide QRS, long QT, hypotension, widened pulse pressure

Risks: old age, additive anticholinergic burden, street drugs / herbals (Datura, angel’s trumpet, PCP, lupin). May worsen angina, CCF, constipation, retention, glaucoma in elderly.

Management

  • Review the whole prescription for anticholinergic burden (e.g. Anticholinergic Burden Scale); first stop last-added / dose-changed agent
  • In elderly with comorbidities: substitute with low/no anticholinergic drugs rather than abruptly stopping
  • Supportive: cooling, IV crystalloid bolus for hypotension, catheter for retention, benzodiazepines for agitation
  • Activated charcoal if overdose <1 h
PhysostigmineMay be considered but risky: worsens asthma, bronchitis, DM, cardiac disease, glaucoma, psychosis.

Quick ruleClonus/hyperreflexia → serotonin · Lead-pipe + slow onset + bradyreflexia → NMS · Dry + dilated + ↓bowel sounds, normal tone → anticholinergic.

Differential diagnosis (all three)

  • Psychiatric: worsening illness, catatonia / malignant catatonia, agitated delirium
  • Infection: meningitis, encephalitis, sepsis, abscess, tetanus, botulism
  • Environmental: heatstroke, head injury
  • Endocrine/metabolic: thyrotoxicosis, pheochromocytoma, ↓Ca, ↓Mg, hypoglycemia
  • Neuro: severe EPS, NCSE, midbrain lesion, stroke
  • Toxic: salicylate, heavy metals, lithium/CBZ/valproate toxicity, strychnine, organophosphate/carbamate
  • Substances: hallucinogens, amphetamine, cocaine, alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal
  • Others: Parkinson hyperpyrexia (stopping antiparkinsonian), reserpine/tetrabenazine, porphyria, SLE, autoimmune encephalitis

Investigations & Supportive Care

Investigations (NMS / SS / anticholinergic)

Core
  • Hemogram (leukocytosis)
  • CPK (≥4× normal supports NMS)
  • Urine myoglobin
  • RFT, electrolytes (Na, K, Ca, PO4), glucose
  • ABG, ECG, LFT, coagulation
As indicated
  • Iron profile (low = poor prognosis in NMS)
  • Blood culture; CSF if meningitis
  • EEG (diffuse slowing)
  • Neuroimaging (encephalitis/abscess)
  • Toxicology screen; chest X-ray (aspiration)
  • Doppler for DVT; autoimmune panel

Supportive measures

  • Stop offending agent; ICU or quiet room; ABC, monitor vitals
  • Hyperthermia: cooling blankets + antipyretics
  • IV fluids; strict input/output; catheter if retention; prevent renal failure in NMS (urine alkalinisation with sodium bicarbonate)
  • Prevent hypoglycemia / nutritional care; correct low iron (NMS)
  • Lorazepam for agitation; treat seizures
  • BP: antihypertensives in SS; crystalloid bolus in anticholinergic hypotension
  • Early mobilisation/physio; heparin if immobile (DVT prophylaxis)
  • Positioning to prevent aspiration

Valproate & Carbamazepine Toxicity

Valproate

Intentional / accidental overdose; mainly neurological.

  • CNS: irritability, ataxia, confusion, coma, hallucinations, myoclonus, miosis, fever/hypothermia
  • CVS/resp: hypotension, tachycardia, arrest, respiratory depression (massive)
  • GI/other: vomiting, hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis
  • Dx: history + ↑transaminases, ↑ammonia, high VPA level

Carbamazepine

Overdose, or interactions (enzyme inhibitors, food). Erratic absorption → delayed symptoms.

  • CNS: sedation, nystagmus, diplopia, ataxia, seizures, myoclonus, coma, mydriasis, resp. depression
  • CVS: hypotension, sinus tachycardia, arrhythmia
  • Other: anticholinergic ileus, rhabdomyolysis, anaemia
  • Dx: serial CBZ levels

Valproate management

  • Stop drug; supportive; benzodiazepines for seizures/agitation; correct electrolytes
  • Activated charcoal 1 g/kg (max 50 g) if <2 h – avoid if sedated / airway unprotected
  • Haemodialysis / extracorporeal if severe: level >1300 mg/L, coma or ventilation, pH <7.10, hyperammonaemic encephalopathy, shock
  • Naloxone 0.8–2 mg (start 0.04 mg IV, titrate) – reverses CNS depression (weak evidence)
  • L-carnitine 100 mg/kg IV over 30 min (max 6 g), then 50 mg/kg (max 3 g) 8-hourly – for hyperammonaemia / hepatotoxicity

Carbamazepine management

  • Dose- and symptom-guided; single activated charcoal if recent (protect airway)
  • Haemodialysis, charcoal haemoperfusion, IV lipid emulsion, CVVHDF in severe cases

Overdose: Antipsychotics, Antidepressants, Benzodiazepines

Initial steps (all)Identify drug, dose, co-ingestants, time since ingestion; ABC; IV access; ECG; ask about suicidality; check empty strips; rule out other causes.

Exaggerated pharmacology: D2 (EPS, NMS), M1 (anticholinergic), H1 (sedation), α1 (hypotension), 5-HT2A.

Benzodiazepine overdose

Usually not fatal in healthy adults; dangerous in elderly, COPD, liver disease, short-acting potent agents (midazolam, triazolam), co-ingestants. Features: drowsiness, ataxia, nystagmus, dysarthria, confusion, coma, hypotension, hypothermia, respiratory depression; paradoxical agitation/aggression; anterograde amnesia.

  • Airway protection, prevent aspiration; decontamination rarely – single charcoal if <1 h, conscious, airway safe
Flumazenil – selective use onlyDose 0.1–0.2 mg/min IV over 30 min, repeat after ≥1 min if needed, max 1–2 mg; infusion to prevent resedation. Get baseline ECG.
Contraindicated: long-term BZD users (therapeutic or abuse), epilepsy, ↑ICP, arrhythmia, prolonged QTc/abnormal ECG. Risks: withdrawal, seizures, SVT. Response is inconsistent.

Quick Revision Sheet

Timelines
  • Dystonia: ≤4 d (95%), ≤7 d
  • NMS: ≤10 d most, ≤30 d nearly all
  • Serotonin syndrome: 6–24 h
  • Restart antipsychotic: ≥2 wk after NMS
  • Restart antidepressant: 1–2 wk after SS
Antidotes / key drugs
  • Dystonia → benztropine / biperiden / diphenhydramine; laryngospasm → BZD
  • Akathisia → propranolol / mirtazapine
  • NMS → bromocriptine, dantrolene, ECT
  • SS → cyproheptadine
  • Anticholinergic → physostigmine (cautious)
  • Valproate → L-carnitine, naloxone
  • BZD → flumazenil (selective)
  • TCA → NaHCO₃, lipid emulsion
  • Prolonged QTc → MgSO₄ 2–4 g
Dialysis triggers
  • Lithium: chronic >2.5; acute >4; neuro/cardiac severity
  • Valproate: severe toxicity (>1300 mg/L, coma, pH <7.10)
Charcoal windows
  • Antipsychotic, lithium WBI, BZD: <1 h
  • Antidepressant: 1–2 h · Valproate: <2 h
Pitfalls
  • Don’t miss laryngeal dystonia, suicidality in akathisia
  • Don’t label before excluding sepsis, CNS infection, delirium, other toxins
  • Succinylcholine in NMS/ECT; opioids in serotonin syndrome; flumazenil in chronic BZD users
  • Abruptly stopping anticholinergic drugs in the elderly can destabilise comorbidities
Self-test: which syndrome?
  • Clonus + diarrhoea + sweating after SSRI+tramadol → Serotonin syndrome
  • Lead-pipe rigidity + fever + ↑CPK after IM haloperidol → NMS
  • Dry flushed skin, dilated pupils, urinary retention after TCA + antihistamine → Anticholinergic
  • Tremor, ataxia on lithium + NSAID → Chronic lithium toxicity
