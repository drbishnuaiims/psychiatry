🧠 Psychiatric Disorders in Parkinson’s Disease

Bedside / grand-round reference · Source: Tripathi, Gupta, Bansal. IPS CPG, Indian J Psychiatry 2022;64(Suppl 2):S330–S343 · Aid only; individualise every patient.

Overview

Parkinson’s disease (PD)Heterogeneous, progressive neurodegenerative synucleinopathy; degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in SNpc. Motor triad: akinesia / bradykinesia, resting tremor (4–6 Hz), rigidity. 2nd commonest neurodegenerative disease after Alzheimer’s.
MotorNon-motor
Resting tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity, postural instability · hypomimia (“masked facies”), soft monotone speech, dysphagia, dysarthria, sialorrhoea · shuffling / festinating gait, ↓ arm swing, difficulty turning or rising, slow ADLs, micrographia · glabellar reflex, striatal deformity, scoliosis, blepharospasm, dystoniaNeuropsychiatric: cognitive impairment, dementia, depression, anxiety, anhedonia, apathy, psychosis, ICDs · Sensory: anosmia, ageusia, pain (shoulder, back), paraesthesia · Dysautonomia: urinary, constipation, orthostatic hypotension, sexual dysfunction, seborrhoea, abnormal sweating, weight loss · Sleep: insomnia, EDS, RBD, RLS, OSA

Psychiatric syndromes covered

DepressionAnxietyPsychosis (PDP)Cognitive impairment / dementiaApathySleep disordersImpulse control disorders (ICDs)
  • Depression ⇄ PD – vicious cycle: either raises risk of the other; but depression does not predict motor progression.
  • Anxiety: 2nd commonest psychiatric disorder; may be a predictor / prodrome (up to 20 years before PD, even after controlling for smoking, caffeine, anxiolytics); under-recognised (symptom overlap).
  • PDP is a separate clinical entity from schizophrenia / acute psychosis; “hallucinations and delusions during PD”; marks staging; poor prognosis, burden, death. NINDS–NIMH: a continuum reflecting disease progression.
  • Cognition: any stage; may precede onset; progressive. Apathy: half have standalone apathy (no depression or cognitive impairment). Sleep: RBD may occur years before PD. ICDs: pleasurable behaviours done repetitively, excessively, compulsively.
  • All impair QoL and ↑ caregiver distress.

Apathy vs depression

ApathyMarked reduction in interest and goal-directed behaviour; lack of initiative; indifference. A motivational disorder (flattened emotion) – depression is an affective disorder. Diagnose apathy, depression and cognitive dysfunction separately.

  • Depression: major 5–20%, minor 10–30%, dysthymia 22.5%.
  • Anxiety types: GAD 14.1% > social phobia 13.8% > anxiety NOS 13.3% > panic ± agoraphobia 6.8%. Anxiety + depression co-occur in up to 80%.
  • PDP: age >50; up to 60% within 12 years; as early as 19 months in 27%. Minor phenomena (hallucinations, visual illusions) 17–72%; auditory / tactile hallucinations and delusions less common.
  • Dementia: point prevalence ~30%; 2–6× controls; cumulative 75–80% at 10-year survival.

Etiopathogenesis

  • Core pathology: loss of SNpc dopaminergic neurons + α-synuclein / Lewy bodies; motor signs after ~60–80% neuronal loss. Cause unclear: oxidative stress, inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, protein handling. Psychiatric causation multifactorial.

Depression, cognition, anxiety, apathyDopaminergic degeneration. Depression: ↓ acetylcholine, serotonin; habenula, limbic and basal ganglia–orbitofrontal dysfunction
AnxietyPsychosocial, medical, neurochemical (↓ dopaminergic / noradrenergic neurons in locus coeruleus and limbic system); “reactive” to diagnosis or to motor limits; due to drugs (levodopa, pergolide). Late afternoon / evening panic-like attacks tied to “off” periods – unique to PD

Differential diagnosis of cognitive impairment

ConditionKey points
PDD vs DLBSame Lewy body spectrum, similar features (cognitive decline + parkinsonism, visual hallucinations, fluctuating attention). Dementia ≥1 year after motor onset = PDD; cognitive impairment before (or with) motor symptoms = DLB. DLB: fluctuating cognition with hallucinations; anxiety, depression, apathy less prominent than PDD
Alzheimer’sMemory, visuospatial, language, executive domains
Toxic-metabolicABG, biochemistry, EEG, CT / MRI
Medication toxicityExcess dopamine replacement → executive / attention problems, hallucinations, delusions. DAs worst; amantadine can too; carbidopa / levodopa least (but high dose can). Trihexyphenidyl particularly detrimental to cognition

Anxiety

  • SSRIs / SNRIs first line (data inconclusive). Avoid TCAs in elderly. Benzodiazepines anecdotally effective but ↑ falls, fractures, cognitive impairment, dependence → lowest dose, shortest time.
  • Secondary anxiety: due to anti-PD drugs → adjust dose, replace if not tolerated; panic in “off” periods → adjust anti-PD drugs to reduce off time (FDA-approved motor-fluctuation drugs).
  • Non-drug: relaxation, social adaptation, sleep hygiene, psychoeducation, CBT (maladaptive thoughts, attacks, situational anxiety).

Management: PD Psychosis

FirstFind and fix modifiable factors: correct vision / hearing, low-stimulation but well-lit room (reduces minor visual hallucinations), preserve circadian rhythm, reassure; rule out delirium, delirium-prone drugs, dehydration and electrolyte imbalance. Aim: cut polypharmacy, use essential drugs optimally; avoid worsening motor / cognitive symptoms.

Apathy

  • No proven drug; dopaminergic rationale. Pramipexole improves decreased willingness; amantadine (↑ dopamine release, catecholamine action); rivastigmine (positive RCT); methylphenidate, amphetamines, bupropion used clinically.

Management: Sleep Disorders & ICDs

Common stepsFind the cause: review drugs, sleep hygiene, comorbidity; sleep log; full medical / neurological history and exam.
DisorderManagement
InsomniaOptimise motor symptoms first (low evening DA dose; slow-release forms). Relaxation, sleep hygiene, CBT before drugs. Zopiclone / eszopiclone have largely replaced BZDs (elderly 3.75 mg → 5–7.5 mg). Doxepin proven in PD insomnia. Melatonin if newer hypnotics / doxepin fail
EDSTreat comorbidity (depression, poor night sleep, fatigue, OSA); lower or stop D2 agonist; modafinil, caffeine, atomoxetine (conflicting results)
RBDStop SSRIs, SNRIs, clomipramine, bisoprolol (can cause RBD). Melatonin first option (3–12 mg/night); clonazepam
RLSLook for secondary causes: iron-deficiency anaemia, neuropathy, myelopathy, diabetes, uraemia. α2δ ligands (gabapentin, pregabalin) mainstay. Dopaminergics (pramipexole, rotigotine, ropinirole, levodopa) now less preferred – oedema, ICDs, EDS, morning rebound. Botulinum toxin mixed
OSARule out RBD (↑ OSA risk); weight loss; CPAP in severe; if CPAP-intolerant, sustained-release levodopa at bedtime (no dosing guideline)

Impulse control disorders

Prevent: assess risks (Table 5) before dopaminergics; psychoeducate patient + family; NICE – informed consent before DA
First step: reduce or stop DA (or switch DA, lower dopaminergics); address modifiable risks
Symptoms may persist ≥12 weeks after stopping; DA withdrawal syndrome and worse motor symptoms possible – individualise
Adjuncts (limited / controversial evidence): SSRIs for mood / anxiety; CBT, caregiver education; valproate, topiramate; clozapine, quetiapine; naltrexone, nalmefene; zonisamide, donepezil, noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor
  • DBS: may cut dopaminergic need and ICD risk (STN stimulation); yet transient de novo ICD after STN-DBS, or late onset, can occur.
  • Higher risk: young age, early onset, longer illness, mood disorder, addictive behaviours, short-acting DAs, DBS, cultural factors.

Quick Revision

Numbers
  • Depression 2.7–55.6%; anxiety 3.6–40%; apathy ≤40%
  • Anxiety + depression up to 80%
  • PDP: 60% by 12 years; criteria ≥1 month
  • Dementia 30% point, 75–80% at 10 y
  • PDD: ≥1 year after motor onset
  • Motor signs: 60–80% neuron loss
  • Pimavanserin 34 mg; clozapine 6.25–50; quetiapine 50–150
Drug choices
  • Depression: sertraline, SNRIs; TCA = nortriptyline / desipramine last
  • PDP: pimavanserin, clozapine; avoid risperidone, olanzapine
  • PDD: rivastigmine (FDA)
  • RBD: melatonin, clonazepam
  • RLS: gabapentin, pregabalin
  • Insomnia: zopiclone, doxepin, melatonin
  • ICD: ↓ DA
Pitfalls
  • Bupropion may precipitate psychosis
  • Trihexyphenidyl, benztropine worsen cognition
  • Rasagiline / selegiline + SSRI → serotonin syndrome
  • ECT → delirium; DBS → ↑ suicide, apathy
  • DAs → ICDs, hallucinations
  • Anxiety in “off” periods – fix motor fluctuation
Self-test
  • Hallucinations with intact insight, sense of someone nearby? → early PDP (minor, presence hallucination); review drugs, lighting, delirium.
  • Dementia onset 6 months before parkinsonism? → DLB, not PDD.
  • New gambling after starting pramipexole? → ICD; reduce / switch DA.
  • Evening panic episodes? → “off” period anxiety; adjust anti-PD regimen.
  • PD with psychosis and dementia? → lower PD drugs, rivastigmine / donepezil; avoid D2 blockers.
