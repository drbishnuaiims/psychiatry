🦋 Psychiatric Disorders & Endocrine Disorders

Bedside / grand-round reference · Source: Mishra, Sawant, Garg. IPS CPG, Indian J Psychiatry 2022;64(Suppl 2):S402–S413 · Aid only; individualise every patient.

Overview

Core ideaBidirectional relationship: endocrine disease → psychiatric symptoms, psychiatric illness → endocrine change, and treatment cuts both ways (lithium → thyroid; SGAs → glucose / metabolic syndrome). Endocrine disease may present first as a psychiatric picture and stay undiagnosed.
  • History: Kraepelin hypothesised dementia praecox as an endocrine disorder (thyroxine lack in early development impairing maturation of behaviour).
  • Covered: thyroid, parathyroid, diabetes, metabolic syndrome, hyperprolactinaemia, steroid-induced psychosis. Also stress–immune–HPA axis; psychiatric presentations: depression, anxiety, cognitive dysfunction, psychosis, delirium.
  • Early diagnosis and referral improve outcomes and coping.

Thyroid Disorders & Psychiatric Illness

  • First confirmed link 1888 (Clinical Society of London committee). Asher (~7 decades ago): psychosis in 14 hypothyroid patients – “myxoedema madness”.
  • Common NPS: depression, anxiety, cognitive difficulty. Screen thyroid function in cognitive disorders; no consensus on population screening, but Garber CPG endorses case-finding in psychiatric disorders.

Hypothyroidism

DefinitionsOvert (OH): ↑ TSH + ↓ T4 and/or T3 with clinical evidence. Subclinical (SCH): ↑ TSH with normal free T4 / T3.
  • Affective disorder probands: mostly euthyroid; 1–4% OH, 4–40% SCH; up to 52% of refractory depression may have SCH. Meta-analysis odds in hypothyroidism: depression 3.56, anxiety 2.32. Up to 25% of female rapid cyclers hypothyroid.
  • NPS (OH): ill-defined, nonspecific, insidious; early attention, concentration, memory loss; later slow processing, can’t do chores. Striking: psychomotor retardation, fatigability; trouble with sustained mental effort, complex questions, new learning; ↓ concern for others; depression; severe: illusions, visual hallucinations, paranoia; psychosis with affective + schizophrenic admixture.
  • DDx depression: slowing, attention problems mimic depression; in OH it is irritable and chronic; psychosis has confusion, persistent cognitive disturbance, VH.
Treat with levothyroxine (up to 1.6 µg/kg/day) → TSH normal usually suffices
Residual NPS in some (e.g. long-standing dementia) – labelling effect / ascertainment bias possible
T3 (not available in India) augmentation: initial reports, later controlled studies inconsistent
Work-up of residual NPS (Table 1): physical exam, OSA screen, metabolic panel, vitamin D, thyroid antibodies, exercise, diet, sleep hygiene, change levothyroxine brand

Subclinical hypothyroidism

  • Frequent depression / anxiety / cognitive symptoms even in young; study inconsistency from TSH upper-limit definitions and age-related rise.
  • Progression to OH risk: female, TSH >10 mIU/L, thyroid antibodies → start LT4. Benefit of LT4 for NPS modest, inconsistent.

Hyperthyroidism

  • ↓ TSH (<0.1 mIU/L), ↑ TH; commonest cause Graves’. Symptoms (hyperadrenergic) resemble primary psychiatric disorders. Abrupt onset.
  • Nervousness, fatigue, restlessness, irritability, overactivity, lability, ↓ frustration tolerance, distractibility. Anxiety up to 80%; depression up to 70% (prolonged disease, noradrenergic exhaustion); early inflated well-being akin to mania; rarely delirium; psychosis uncommon (mixed affective / schizophrenia-like). T4 correlates with anxiety, not depression.
  • Elderly: “apathetic hyperthyroidism” – depression, lethargy, mental slowing, no eye signs.
  • DDx anxiety: heat intolerance (prefers cold), eye signs (lid lag), ↑ appetite with weight loss (anxiety: ↓ appetite); stress precipitation more exclusive to anxiety.
  • Treatment: euthyroid state → 50% complete, 35% partial NPS response; extra psychotropics for residual. Propranolol 60–80 mg – rapid relief even before euthyroidism; blocks T4→T3 conversion.

Non-thyroidal illness / refractory depression

  • Systemic illness: ↓ T3, T4, suppressed TSH. Acute psychosis / mood: ↑ T4 (hyperthyroxinaemia); substance use: ↑ TSH. Mechanism unknown – “red herring” abnormal TFTs.
  • Depression: subtle changes – ↑ TSH, altered TH circadian rhythm, blunted TSH response to TRH. Upper-quartile normal TSH ↔ recurrent, severe, poorly responsive depression. D2 deiodinase Thr92Ala polymorphism → brain hypothyroidism despite normal TSH.
  • T4 / T3 (T3 better studied) added to augment or accelerate antidepressants even without biochemical dysfunction; with SCH / OH → supplement in refractory depression or rapid cycling.

Other psychotropics

  • Carbamazepine: ↓ free T4 within 2 months, reversible, no clinical hypothyroidism, no special monitoring. Valproate + CBZ → ↓ free T4, ↑ TSH → monitor thyroid.
  • TCAs, phenothiazines: drug–iodide complexes → hypothyroid state; regular TFT.
  • Prolactin-raising antipsychotics may derange TFT more.
  • Euthyroid hyperthyroxinaemia (high T4, normal TSH): methadone, stimulants. Benzodiazepines: no effect.

Parathyroid Disorders

Hyperparathyroidism

  • ~0.1% population; commoner in older women. PHPT: ↑ PTH, ↑ Ca, hypophosphataemia. Hypercalcaemia fatigue / lethargy / anorexia mimic psychiatric illness; insidious → coma. “Moans (abdominal cramps), bones, stones”.
  • Causes: neck radiation; lithium (~10% hypercalcaemia; fewer renal stones). Think hypercalcaemia in lithium-treated patients with atypical features or non-response.
  • NPS: depression (to 62%), anxiety (to 53%), irritability (to 51%), apathy, fatigue (“psychiatric overtones” → mild–moderate hypercalcaemia). Cognitive symptoms mainly elderly. Delirium and coma at Ca >14 mg/dL. Psychosis rare; weak link with major NCD in long-standing disease; chronic → seclusion, withdrawal.
  • Treatment: surgical parathyroidectomy for overt hypercalcaemic PHPT with NPS; NPS improves unless chronic. Mild PHPT with NPS: surgery not recommended (international workshop); benefit uncertain.

Hypoparathyroidism

  • Commonest cause iatrogenic (neck surgery). Hypocalcaemia: neuromuscular irritability, cramps, paraesthesia, facial grimacing, seizures.
  • NPS in ~half post-surgery (more in idiopathic): post-op delirium, anxiety, depression, lability, irritability, socially awkward behaviour, nervousness; psychosis in clear consciousness uncommon; cognitive deficits worsen with chronicity. Basal ganglia calcification → poor QoL, more refractory NPS.
  • Associated with velocardiofacial (22q11.2 deletion) syndrome → schizophrenia and mood disorder in adults. “Partial insufficiency” → episodic depression / anxiety. Anxiety → hyperventilation → tetany.
  • Treatment: correct biochemistry (helps non-chronic cases); delirium resolves spontaneously; depression / anxiety usually remit; short-term BZD if debilitating; ~half of cognitive impairment improves unless intracranial calcification / long-standing. Enhanced neuroleptic sensitivity reported.
RuleOrganic syndromes of thyroid / parathyroid disease need only endocrine treatment unless NPS persists after 4 weeks of adequate treatment or is severe.

Diabetes & Psychiatric Illness

422 millionworldwide with diabetes
77 millionadults in India; 101 M by 2030, 134 M by 2045
19% / 15%urban / rural diabetes + prediabetes India
43.9 millionundiagnosed in India (2nd to China 65.2 M)
  • ≈⅓ cannot self-manage. Psychological status affects glycaemic control, complications, disability, mortality. Psychotropics add to T2DM prevalence. Link in both type 1 and type 2.
  • Stress: mechanism unclear; association with poor glycaemic control.

Depression ⇄ diabetes

  • Lustman: reciprocal – hyperglycaemia ↔ depression; strong HbA1c association. Depression rates 2–3× general population.
  • Method issue: PHQ without interview over-estimates. INTERPRET-DD (14 countries): MINI major depression 10.6% vs PHQ moderate–severe 17%; minor depression 13% (Golden).
  • Poor motivation, energy, hopelessness → non-adherence → worse control and complications.
  • Biology: insulin resistance, ↑ cytokines (pro-inflammatory), ↑ cortisol / counter-regulatory hormones (HPA) → insulin resistance; poor antidepressant response with high inflammatory markers. Lifestyle: inactivity, high-calorie fatty food, smoking, drinking.

Assessment (Fig 4)

  • Psychiatric history; risk factors / family history; BMI, weight
  • Fasting + post-prandial glucose, HbA1c; screen cardiac, renal, eye complications
  • BDI, PHQ-9; diabetes-specific QoL / self-care burden; cognitive decline from chronic ischaemic change; MRI brain if needed

  • All antidepressants safe; improved depression → better self-care and control.
  • CBT: good for mild depression; ↓ diabetes distress; short-term ↓ HbA1c; changes negative illness beliefs / self-care; short-term > long-term benefit; untailored CBT improves depression more than distress.
  • Internet-guided self-help: short-term benefit, easily integrated in diabetes self-care.
  • Lifestyle: healthy eating, exercise, yoga, relaxation, breathing.

Anxiety, SMI, Eating Disorders, Sexual & Cognitive Aspects

Anxiety

  • Common in T2DM: GAD, panic, many subsyndromal. Worse self-care, control, complications; fear of hypoglycaemia (Rechenberg, 15 studies) affects monitoring and QoL.
  • T1DM interventions: week-long summer camp (↑ attitude, ↓ trait anxiety); CBT (restructure cognitions, problem-solving); continuous glucose monitoring did not change anxiety. Treat early.

Bipolar disorder & schizophrenia

  • Bipolar: diabetes 10–26% (weight gain, obesity, shared genetics, comorbidity). Monitor, lifestyle.
  • Schizophrenia: ↑ diabetes risk – sedentary, nicotine, ↑ appetite, high-carb food, SGA effects (dyslipidaemia, glucose intolerance, insulin resistance, weight gain) → MetS.

Eating disorders (diabulimia)

  • Intensive control → weight gain, food / portion / glucose preoccupation, esp. women with T1DM. Insulin omission / manipulation = purging equivalent → DKA, retinopathy, higher HbA1c. ~7% of adolescents with T1DM. Often missed (no vomiting / laxatives); restriction and binge eating common; classical anorexia rare. T2DM: recurrent binge eating with obesity.
Multidisciplinary: endocrinologist / diabetologist, nutritionist with ED experience, mental health professional, counsellor
Medical stabilisation: ↑ insulin dose, ↑ food intake, flexible meal plan, regular eating, glucose monitoring
Assess comorbid psychopathology · CBT for insulin omission · antidepressants

Sexual and cognitive

  • Sexual: men – ED, ↓ interest, ejaculatory problems (age, duration, complications); women – ↓ libido, arousal, lubrication. See the psychosexual CPG.
  • Children / adolescents with T1DM (hyper- / hypoglycaemia): learning difficulty, ↓ processing speed, attention deficit, short-term memory problems – assess regularly, counsel parents. Adults T2DM: chronic hyperglycaemia, micro- / macro-vascular brain change → cognitive decline, dementia; regular glucose monitoring is the only prevention.
  • Annual in T2DM: complete physical exam, foot exam, eye check.

Metabolic Syndrome (MetS)

A condition (not a disease) of cardiometabolic problems from lifestyle change + genetic vulnerability; raised cardiac mortality. ↑ risk in schizophrenia, depression, bipolar. Components: abdominal obesity, insulin resistance / glucose intolerance, ↑ TG with ↓ HDL, hypertension.

Steroid-induced psychosis

  • DSM-5: substance / medication-induced psychotic disorder. Criteria: exposure to a drug that can cause delusions / hallucinations; not better explained by another psychotic disorder; not only during delirium; causes distress / impairment.
  • Severe NPS (incl. psychosis) ≈ 6%. Strongest risk factor: dose within first 2 weeks. May occur any time, even after stopping. Pathophysiology unclear – glucocorticoid over mineralocorticoid receptor stimulation.
  • Prevention: lower dose, shorter course. Treatment: reassure; reduce / stop steroid; add antipsychotic – haloperidol most used.

Quick Revision

Numbers
  • OH 1–4%, SCH 4–40% in affective disorder; 52% refractory depression SCH
  • Li: hypothyroid 8–19%, SCH ≤23%, goitre 2.5–50%, ↑ Ca ~10%
  • SCH → treat if TSH >10
  • LT4 ≤1.6 µg/kg/day; propranolol 60–80 mg
  • Delirium / coma Ca >14 mg/dL
  • Depression in DM 2–3×; DM in bipolar 10–26%
  • MetS: clozapine 47.2% … aripiprazole 19.4%
  • Steroid psychosis ~6%
Do
  • TSH ± calcium baseline and 6-monthly on lithium
  • Check thyroid in refractory depression / rapid cycling
  • Glucose, lipids, weight before and during antipsychotics
  • Baseline prolactin; MRI if persistently high
  • Think hypercalcaemia in atypical lithium patients
Pitfalls
  • Don’t stop lithium for hypothyroidism
  • Hyperthyroid ≠ anxiety: appetite ↑, weight ↓, heat intolerance
  • Elderly apathetic hyperthyroidism looks like depression
  • PHQ alone over-diagnoses depression in DM
  • Insulin omission = diabulimia; DKA risk
  • Calcified basal ganglia → refractory NPS
Self-test
  • Elderly lethargic “depressed” patient, weight loss, tachycardia? → Apathetic hyperthyroidism.
  • Lithium patient, TSH 12, euthymic? → Levothyroxine, continue lithium.
  • Bipolar on lithium, new lethargy and constipation, no response? → check calcium / PTH.
  • Overweight schizophrenia with diabetes risk, choose antipsychotic? → ziprasidone / lurasidone / aripiprazole; avoid olanzapine, clozapine.
  • Young T1DM woman with rising HbA1c and weight fear? → Diabulimia.
